Missed-call text back is an automatic text sent to a person who just called your business and didn't reach anyone. The text says it's an automated assistant for your business, asks what they need, can share your booking link, and includes "Reply STOP to opt out." It goes only to numbers that called you. It never goes to lists or cold contacts.

How it works

  1. A customer calls and you can't pick up.
  2. Within moments, the system texts that number back from your business line.
  3. The customer replies with what they need, or books with your link.
  4. You get an alert with the details, and you or your team call back.

Three sample texts

1) Hi, this is the automated assistant for {Your Company}. Sorry we missed your call! What can we help with? Reply STOP to opt out.

2) Hi, it's the automated assistant for {Your Company}. We're on a job right now. Reply with your address and the problem, or book here: {link}. Reply STOP to opt out.

3) Thanks for calling {Your Company}. This is our automated assistant. We're closed now and someone will reply when we open. If anyone is in danger, call 911. Reply STOP to opt out.

Sample wording for a fictional business.

Does it work after hours?

Yes. The text goes out whenever a call is missed. Say clearly when a person will follow up, and point emergencies to 911 or your emergency line.

Consent and carrier basics

  • Only people who called you. ReadySite texts back only a number that just called your business, or one that's in a conversation it started. No cold texts, no bought lists, no list uploads.
  • What the FCC says. FCC rules ban autodialed texts to a mobile phone unless the owner gave consent first or the text is for an emergency. Commercial texts require written consent, while consent for informational texts can be oral. People can opt out of any robotext at any time, in any reasonable way (FCC: stop unwanted robocalls and texts; FCC: TCPA rules on revoking consent). Keep the reply about the caller's own request, and don't add promotions.
  • Opt-out every time. Every first text includes "Reply STOP to opt out," which matches carrier industry guidance (CTIA Messaging Principles), and STOP is honored right away.
  • Carrier registration. Business texting from a regular local number needs A2P 10DLC registration (The Campaign Registry). ReadySite handles that during setup.

This is general information, not legal advice. Whether your texts need consent, and what kind, depends on your facts. Ask your own attorney.
